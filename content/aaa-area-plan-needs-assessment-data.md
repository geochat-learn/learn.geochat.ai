faq:
  - q: "What does the Older Americans Act require in an area plan needs assessment?"
    a: "Under 45 CFR 1321.65(b)(3), as revised in 2024, each AAA submits objectively collected and, where possible, statistically valid data with conclusions on unmet need. It covers supportive, nutrition, disease prevention and health promotion, family caregiver and senior center services, whatever their funding source. Section 1321.65(b)(2) also requires identifying populations in greatest economic and greatest social need."
  - q: "Which Census table shows older adults living alone?"
    a: "ACS table B09021, Living Arrangements of Adults 18 Years and Over by Age, has a '65 years and over: Lives alone' line (variable B09021_023). Table B11007 counts one-person households with someone 65 or older. Neither splits at 60, so label the result 65+."
  - q: "Where can an AAA get free data on older adults by ZIP code?"
    a: "ACS 5-year Detailed Tables are published for ZIP Code Tabulation Areas (ZCTAs), which approximate ZIP codes; the 2020–2024 estimates use 2020 ZCTA boundaries. To match ZIP-coded client records to tracts or counties, use HUD's quarterly HUD-USPS ZIP crosswalk files."
  - q: "How did the 2024 rule define greatest social need?"
    a: "45 CFR 1321.3 defines it as need caused by noneconomic factors: physical and mental disabilities, language barriers, and cultural, social or geographical isolation. Isolation causes listed include racial or ethnic status, Native American identity, HIV status, chronic conditions, lack of transportation and rural location. States and AAAs may add other needs."
  - q: "Which ACS table comes closest to 'at risk for institutional placement'?"
    a: "Table B18106 counts people with a self-care difficulty, meaning difficulty bathing or dressing, with 65–74 and 75+ age groups. The Act's definition uses inability to perform at least two activities of daily living without substantial assistance, so present the ACS figure as a proxy."

An AAA area plan needs assessment should use ACS 5-year data on older adults, CMS and HRSA service locations, and local data on unmet need. Federal rules set the minimum, and your state unit on aging sets the format, the definitions and often the data list.

## What the Older Americans Act requires

ACL's 2024 update to the Older Americans Act (OAA) regulations [took effect March 15, 2024, and regulated entities had until October 1, 2025 to comply](https://acl.gov/sites/default/files/oam/2024/OAA_FinalRuleOverview2024.pdf). The rule was [published in the Federal Register on February 14, 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-02-14/pdf/2024-01913.pdf) and revised 45 CFR Part 1321.

### Area plan rules in 45 CFR 1321.65

| Section | What it requires | What it means for data |
|---|---|---|
| § 1321.65(b)(2) | Identify populations in the planning and service area at [greatest economic need and greatest social need](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-B/chapter-XIII/subchapter-C/part-1321/subpart-C/section-1321.65), including those in the § 1321.3 definitions | Map each defined group, not only total older adults |
| § 1321.65(b)(3) | Submit ["objectively collected, and where possible, statistically valid, data with evaluative conclusions concerning the unmet need"](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-B/chapter-XIII/subchapter-C/part-1321/subpart-C/section-1321.65) for supportive, nutrition, disease prevention and health promotion, family caregiver, and senior center services | Count all services in these categories, [regardless of funding source](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-B/chapter-XIII/subchapter-C/part-1321/subpart-C/section-1321.65) |
| § 1321.65(b)(4) | Seek views from older adults, caregivers, providers and the public, with [at least 30 calendar days for review and comment](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-B/chapter-XIII/subchapter-C/part-1321/subpart-C/section-1321.65) unless the State waives it | Share draft maps during the comment period |
| § 1321.65(c) | Include services for [hunger, food insecurity and malnutrition; social isolation; and physical and mental health conditions](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-B/chapter-XIII/subchapter-C/part-1321/subpart-C/section-1321.65) | Add food, isolation and health indicators |

The statute adds detail. [Section 306(a)(1)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t42/s3026/) has area plans weigh the number of older individuals in several groups. These are people with low incomes, in greatest economic or social need, at risk for institutional placement, and who are Indians.

[Section 306(a)(4)(A)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t42/s3026/) requires specific objectives for low-income minority older individuals, those with limited English proficiency, and rural residents. The Act defines an older individual as [someone 60 years of age or older](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2018-title42/pdf/USCODE-2018-title42-chap35.pdf).

### Greatest economic and social need (45 CFR 1321.3)

[Section 1321.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/1321.3) defines greatest economic need as income at or below the federal poverty level, as further defined by State and area plans. Those plans may weigh local factors, including geography and expenses.

Greatest social need is need caused by noneconomic factors. [The listed factors](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/1321.3) are physical and mental disabilities, language barriers, and cultural, social or geographical isolation, including isolation due to:

- racial or ethnic status, Native American identity, or religious affiliation
- sexual orientation, gender identity or sex characteristics, or HIV status
- chronic conditions
- housing instability, food insecurity, lack of reliable clean water, lack of transportation, or utility assistance needs
- interpersonal safety concerns
- rural location
- any other status that restricts routine daily tasks or threatens independent living

State and area plans may also [add other needs based on local and individual factors](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/1321.3).

Each State plan must state [how the State defines both terms](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-B/chapter-XIII/subchapter-C/part-1321/subpart-B/section-1321.27), and those definitions must include the § 1321.3 populations. Read your state's definition before you pick income cutoffs.

### What three states ask for

State guidance varies more than the federal rule:

- **New Mexico.** The [SFY 2026–2030 area plan guide](https://www.aging.nm.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FY26-30-Area-Plan-Guidance.ADA_.pdf) asks for one table per county with total and 60+ population. Its 60+ rows cover race and ethnicity, poverty, low-income minority, limited English, rural, Alzheimer's and living alone. They also cover grandparents raising grandchildren, disability, veterans, institutional risk and LGBT status. It defines greatest economic need as [income below 185 percent of the federal poverty level](https://www.aging.nm.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FY26-30-Area-Plan-Guidance.ADA_.pdf).
- **South Carolina.** The [2026 area plan program instruction](https://aging.sc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Manuals/Program%20Instructions_AreaPlanProcess_2026.pdf) requires "objectively collected and statistically valid data" but names no data source. It asks for 10-year population change, including the 85+ group, and at least three in-person public events. AAAs must comply by July 1, 2027.
- **California.** The Department of Aging's [planning page](https://aging.ca.gov/Providers_and_Partners/Area_Agencies_on_Aging/Planning) posts its 2024–2028 Area Plan Guidance, a Needs Assessment Guidance document and a 2025 service data dictionary. CDA also publishes [key characteristics of Californians 60 and over](https://lab.data.ca.gov/dataset/key-characteristics-of-californians-age-60-and-over1/8da40538-7370-47e9-8aa7-b91752e2b7fa) by county and planning and service area. Its fields include lives alone, low income, minority, non-English speaking and rural.

## How to map older adults living alone far from services

1. **Use census tracts as the base unit.** The 5-year release covers tracts [for all data tables](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/news/data-releases/2024/release.html). Label maps with town names, and add ZCTA views only when partners work in ZIP codes.
2. **Pull living-alone counts.** For each tract, download B09021 rows for [all 65+](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/variables/B09021_022M.html) and [65+ living alone](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/variables/B09021_023M.html), with margins of error. Map both the count and the share.
3. **Test reliability.** Published ACS margins of error use a [90 percent confidence level](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/publications/2019/acs/acs_aian_handbook_2019_ch07.pdf). Divide each MOE by 1.645 for the standard error, compute the coefficient of variation, and flag tracts above your cutoff.
4. **Build the service layer.** Geocode senior centers, meal sites and case management offices from your contract records. Add CMS home health and nursing home addresses and Eldercare Locator sites.
5. **Measure travel time.** Draw drive-time areas around each site. The free openrouteservice plan allows [500 isochrone requests per day](https://openrouteservice.org/plans/), as of November 2026. Record your threshold in the methods; see [drive-time access](/glossary/drive-time-access).
6. **Overlay need and distance.** Flag tracts with many older adults living alone whose centers fall outside every drive-time area. Use [B25045](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/variables/B25045_006MA.html) no-vehicle households and RUCA codes as second filters.
7. **Check home health reach separately.** Count the agencies listing each ZIP code in the CMS ZIP file. A listed ZIP shows past service, not open capacity.
8. **Test the map with residents.** Bring flagged areas to public sessions and ask where people go for help. Record the answers as primary data for your § 1321.65(b)(3) conclusions on unmet need.

## Common mistakes

- **Labeling 65+ data as the OAA population.** The Act counts people 60 and older. ACS age splits for living alone, disability, language and vehicles start at 65, so label those indicators 65+.
- **Ignoring margins of error in small areas.** Tract estimates for small groups, such as grandparents raising grandchildren, can be unreliable. The Census Bureau suggests [aggregating geographic areas](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/publications/2019/acs/acs_aian_handbook_2019_ch07.pdf) for larger samples.
- **Treating ZIP codes as areas.** ZIP Codes are point-based; ZCTAs are [generalized areal representations](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/geography/guidance/geo-areas/zctas.html), and not every ZIP Code has a ZCTA. HUD's [ZIP–tract and ZIP–county crosswalks](https://www.huduser.gov/PORTAL/node/6572) are updated quarterly. See [ZCTA vs. ZIP code](/glossary/zcta-vs-zip-code).
- **Stopping at the county.** County tables hide which towns are far from services. Analyze tracts, then roll results up for county templates such as New Mexico's.
- **Comparing overlapping or mixed data years.** The Census Bureau says [not to compare overlapping 5-year datasets](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/guidance/comparing-acs-data.html), and to compare 5-year estimates only with 5-year estimates. Note each CMS file's release month beside the ACS years.
- **Counting registrations or listings as staff.** A ZIP in the home health file means a patient was served there, not that staff are free. NPPES provider counts are registrations, not full-time clinicians; see [provider-to-population ratio](/glossary/provider-to-population-ratio).
- **Treating ACS disability as ADL need.** ACS self-care difficulty means [difficulty bathing or dressing](https://www.census.gov/topics/health/disability/guidance/data-collection-acs.html). The Act's "at risk for institutional placement" means [being unable to perform at least 2 activities of daily living](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2018-title42/pdf/USCODE-2018-title42-chap35.pdf) without substantial assistance. Call the ACS figure a proxy.
