A Health Professional Shortage Area (HPSA) is a HRSA designation for a shortage of primary care, dental or mental health providers ([HRSA](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/workforce-shortage-areas/shortage-designation)). The shortage can apply to a geographic area, a population group or a facility.

## Types of HPSA

| Type | What is short | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Geographic | Providers for everyone in a defined area | Service areas include counties, groups of contiguous counties, parts of counties and metropolitan neighborhoods ([42 CFR Part 5, App. A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/appendix-A_to_part_5)) |
| Population | Providers for a specific group within a defined area | Applies only to that group |
| Facility | Providers for a facility's patients | Correctional facilities, state or county mental hospitals, other public or nonprofit facilities, and auto-HPSAs |

Auto-HPSAs are facilities designated by statute or regulation. They include FQHCs, FQHC Look-Alikes, Indian Health facilities, IHS and tribal hospitals, and dual-funded health centers or tribal clinics. CMS-certified Rural Health Clinics in non-urbanized areas qualify if they meet NHSC site requirements ([HRSA](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/workforce-shortage-areas/shortage-designation)).

## How HPSAs are designated and scored

State Primary Care Offices submit applications through HRSA's Shortage Designation Management System (SDMS) ([HRSA](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/workforce-shortage-areas/shortage-designation/reviewing-applications)). The criteria are in the [appendices to 42 CFR Part 5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-5/appendix-Appendix%20A%20to%20Part%205).

For primary care, a geographic area qualifies at a population-to-FTE primary care physician ratio of at least 3,500:1 ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-5/appendix-Appendix%20A%20to%20Part%205)). An area between 3,000:1 and 3,500:1 qualifies if it has unusually high needs or insufficient capacity. Providers in contiguous areas must also be overutilized, excessively distant or inaccessible.

For dental and mental health thresholds, see [provider-to-population ratio](/glossary/provider-to-population-ratio).

After designation, HRSA calculates a score ([HRSA](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/workforce-shortage-areas/shortage-designation/scoring)):

| Discipline | Score range | Components (maximum points) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary care | 0–25 | Population-to-provider ratio (10); population below 100% FPL (5); infant health index (5); travel time to nearest care outside the HPSA (5) |
| Dental | 0–26 | Ratio (10); population below 100% FPL (10); water fluoridation (1); travel time (5) |
| Mental health | 0–25 | Ratio (7); population below 100% FPL (5); elderly ratio (3); youth ratio (3); alcohol abuse (1); substance abuse (1); travel time (5) |

HRSA "measures the relative need of HPSAs and assigns each HPSA a score" ([NHSC](https://nhsc.hrsa.gov/loan-repayment/funding-priorities)). The National Health Service Corps reviews loan repayment applications by descending HPSA score ([NHSC](https://nhsc.hrsa.gov/loan-repayment/funding-priorities)).

## Where to look them up

[HPSA Find](https://data.hrsa.gov/tools/shortage-area/hpsa-find) on data.hrsa.gov searches by location or HPSA ID and filters by discipline, status, type, score and rural status. It also shows Maternity Care Target Area (MCTA) scores ([HRSA](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/workforce-shortage-areas/shortage-designation/scoring)).

Section 332 of the Public Health Service Act requires an annual Federal Register list of HPSAs. The 2026 notice covers HPSAs designated as of April 30, 2026 ([Federal Register](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-13309.pdf)).

## Common pitfalls

- **Ignoring status.** Designations flagged in the September 2025 National Shortage Designation Update are "proposed for withdrawal" ([HRSA](https://www.hrsa.gov/about/news/press-releases/extended-timeline-health-professional-shortage-area-designations)). They remain HPSAs until final withdrawal decisions in a future Federal Register notice. The next annual notice is [scheduled on or before July 1, 2027](https://www.hrsa.gov/about/news/press-releases/extended-timeline-health-professional-shortage-area-designations). Report them separately.
- **Mismatched boundaries.** The 2025 update included changes tied to 2020 Census geographies ([HRSA](https://www.hrsa.gov/about/news/press-releases/extended-timeline-health-professional-shortage-area-designations)). Join HPSA records to boundary files of the same vintage.
- **Reading a population HPSA as area-wide.** It applies to one group; a facility HPSA describes one site.
- **Comparing scores across disciplines.** Dental scores run to 26, and each discipline weights factors differently.
- **Confusing HPSAs with MUA/Ps.** They use different tests and feed different programs; see [MUA/MUP](/glossary/mua-mup).

## Using HPSAs in needs assessments

1. Download HPSA Find records for your area by discipline, keeping the status field.
2. Map geographic HPSAs beside your own [provider-to-population ratios](/glossary/provider-to-population-ratio) and [drive times](/glossary/drive-time-access).
3. Cite the HPSA ID, discipline, type, score and status in your report.
4. Note program eligibility. Geographic primary care HPSAs feed NHSC, Nurse Corps, IHS loan repayment, CMS HPSA bonus payments, Rural Health Clinics and J-1 visa waivers ([HRSA](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/workforce-shortage-areas/shortage-designation)).
