A PHAB community health assessment (CHA) should map four things: population, health status, access to primary and behavioral care, and subpopulation differences. PHAB's draft 2026 template suggests maps for population and health status, including differences across populations or places. It does not list maps as a required element.

This guide links each map to the wording PHAB uses. For the wider CHA process, geographies and margins of error, see our [community health assessment mapping guide](/community-health-assessment-mapping-guide).

## What PHAB actually asks for

PHAB's [Standards & Measures (2026)](https://phaboard.org/accreditation-recognition/standards-measures/) took effect on July 1, 2026. The [Initial Accreditation 2026 document](https://phaboard.org/resource-library/standards-measures-for-initial-accreditation-2026/) is, in PHAB's words, the "official requirements" for initial accreditation.

The measure for the assessment is 1.1.1 A, "Develop a community health assessment."

PHAB's [Health Assessment Template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx) refers to "required elements a–f." It does not list them. Check the elements in the Standards & Measures document itself.

The template, dated as a draft of September 2, 2026, spells out what the assessment should contain. It is published on PHAB's [templates page](https://phaboard.org/resource-library/health-assessment-improvement-planning-templates/). Among its "must" lines:

- "The assessment must include comprehensive, broad-based information drawn from primary data" and "at least two different secondary data sources" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)).
- "The health department must be an active participant in the assessment, even when another organization leads the process" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)).
- "The health assessment must be current within the applicable six-year documentation period" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)).
- For Initial Accreditation, the assessment and key findings "must be actively shared" with "both the public and organizations outside the assessment partnership" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)).

Other lines are instructions inside the template. Examples: "Include at least four distinct demographic variables" and "At a minimum, review access to primary care and behavioral health care" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)).

### Where the template mentions maps

Two section instructions in the template suggest maps. Appendix D also lists "maps or visualizations" among example supporting files. None makes maps required:

- Population section: "Add maps, charts, or short narratives that help readers understand differences across neighborhoods, regions, ages" and other characteristics ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)).
- Health status section: "Include maps, charts, or other visualizations when they help readers understand trends, comparisons" and "differences across populations or places" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)).
- Appendix D: example supporting files for Measure 1.1.1 A include "analyses, maps or visualizations" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)).

The access section uses geographic wording but never says "map."

It lists "the number and geographic distribution of providers, provider-to-population ratios" as relevant information ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)). A map is the plainest way to show a geographic distribution.

## The core map set, requirement by requirement

The table pairs each template line with a map and a public data source. All ACS tables are from the 2020–2024 5-year estimates.

| PHAB template wording | Suggested map | Public data source |
|---|---|---|
| "Identify the state, Tribe, county, city, region, territory, installation, or other geography covered" | Reference map of the jurisdiction, with the tracts or ZCTAs you will use | Census boundaries matched to your data |
| "Include at least four distinct demographic variables" | One map per variable: older adults, race and ethnicity, poverty, disability, limited English | ACS [B01001](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B01001.html), [B03002](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B03002.html), [B17001](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B17001.html), [B18101](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B18101.html), [C16002](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/C16002.html) |
| "Identify the measures, data sources, years, and geographic level" | Tract or ZCTA maps of chronic disease and health behaviors | [CDC PLACES](https://www.cdc.gov/places/about/index.html), plus state vital records |
| Social conditions tied to a "health outcome, behavior, population, or geographic pattern" | Households with no vehicle; people with no health insurance | ACS [B08201](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B08201.html), [B27010](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B27010.html) |
| "At a minimum, review access to primary care and behavioral health care" | Primary care and mental health HPSA boundaries; health center sites | [HRSA data downloads](https://data.hrsa.gov/data/download) |
| "the number and geographic distribution of providers, provider-to-population ratios" | Providers per 10,000 residents by county or ZCTA | [NPPES downloadable file](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/regulations-guidance/administrative-simplification/data-dissemination) with ACS population |
| "differences in outcomes experienced by at least two subpopulations" | Side-by-side maps of one outcome for two groups or two kinds of place | Local data broken out by group; ACS for group locations |
| Optional Appendix E, for regional assessments: "Identify local assets, relationships, resources, or strengths relevant to the findings" | Asset map of clinics, partners and community resources | HRSA health center sites, your partner roster |

Template quotes in the table are from PHAB's [Health Assessment Template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx).

## Population maps

The template suggests disaggregating "when possible and meaningful" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)). Pick variables that relate to your findings, not just the easiest four.

| Variable | ACS table | Lines to use | Universe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age 65 and over | [B01001](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B01001.html) Sex by Age | (_020E to _025E + _044E to _049E) ÷ _001E | Total population |
| Race and ethnicity | [B03002](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B03002.html) Hispanic or Latino Origin by Race | _003E to _009E and _012E, each ÷ _001E | Total population |
| Poverty | [B17001](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B17001.html) Poverty Status by Sex by Age | _002E ÷ _001E | Population for whom poverty status is determined |
| Disability | [B18101](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B18101.html) Sex by Age by Disability Status | "With a disability" lines ÷ _001E | Civilian noninstitutionalized population |
| Limited English | [C16002](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/C16002.html) Household Limited English Speaking Status | _004E, _007E, _010E, _013E ÷ _001E | Households |

Note the universes. Limited English in C16002 counts households, not people, so label the map that way.

B18101 splits older adults into [65 to 74 and 75 and over](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B18101.html). That lets you map disability among the oldest residents separately.

## Health status maps

[CDC PLACES](https://www.cdc.gov/places/about/index.html) gives model-based estimates for 40 measures at county, place, census tract and ZCTA level. It is the usual source for tract-level chronic disease maps. See [how to combine CDC PLACES and Census data on one map](/combine-cdc-places-census-data-map).

Three PLACES limits shape a CHA:

- Tract and ZCTA estimates are not age-adjusted. CDC provides [age-adjusted estimates only at county and place level](https://www.cdc.gov/places/faqs/index.html).
- CDC says model-based estimates [should not be used to evaluate the effect of local public health programs](https://www.cdc.gov/places/faqs/index.html).
- An estimate is [not reported when the area's population count is less than 50](https://www.cdc.gov/places/faqs/index.html).

Label each map with the measure, source, data year and geographic level. The template asks you to identify exactly those four things ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)).

## Access-to-care maps

The template's access section has four parts: health care sector partners, populations experiencing barriers, availability and gaps in services, and conclusions about barriers ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)). It ties this review to Measure 7.1.1 A, "Engage with health care delivery system partners to assess access to health care services."

Maps carry most of the "availability and gaps" part. Build at least these:

1. **Shortage designations.** HRSA's [shortage area data](https://data.hrsa.gov/topics/health-workforce/shortage-areas) covers HPSAs and MUA/Ps. Its [data downloads](https://data.hrsa.gov/data/download) include Area HPSA Designation Boundaries and MUA/P Designation Boundaries as shapefiles. Map primary care and mental health HPSAs separately. See [HPSA](/glossary/hpsa) and [MUA/MUP](/glossary/mua-mup).
2. **Safety-net sites.** The same page offers Health Center Service Delivery and Look-Alike Sites as [XLSX and CSV](https://data.hrsa.gov/data/download). Plot them as points over the HPSA layer.
3. **Provider ratios.** Count primary care and behavioral health providers from the [NPPES full replacement monthly NPI file](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/regulations-guidance/administrative-simplification/data-dissemination). Exclude deactivated NPIs, which CMS says the file [will now include](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/regulations-guidance/administrative-simplification/data-dissemination). NPPES counts are registrations, not full-time clinicians. See [provider-to-population ratio](/glossary/provider-to-population-ratio) and [NPI taxonomy codes](/glossary/npi-taxonomy-codes).
4. **Barriers.** Map households with no vehicle ([B08201](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B08201.html) _002E ÷ _001E) and people with no health insurance ([B27010](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/groups/B27010.html)). B27010 reports no coverage in four age groups: _017E, _033E, _050E and _066E.
5. **Travel time.** Add drive-time areas around clinics if transportation is a theme. See [drive-time access](/glossary/drive-time-access).

The template also lists characteristics to break out: "geography, age, race or ethnicity, insurance status, disability, language, income, education" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)). The population maps above cover most of these.

## Disparity maps

The template asks you to "Clearly describe major health issues and the differences in outcomes experienced by at least two subpopulations" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)). A subpopulation can be a group of people or a kind of place, such as rural areas.

PLACES cannot do this by group on its own. CDC says it [does not include any stratifications by race and ethnicity](https://www.cdc.gov/places/faqs/index.html). Its estimates are also not split by age, sex or poverty.

Use one of these approaches instead:

- **Place-based comparison.** Map one PLACES measure, then compare high-poverty and low-poverty tracts. CDC notes that tract differences [may be somewhat reflective of racial and ethnic disparities](https://www.cdc.gov/places/faqs/index.html) where tracts are fairly homogeneous. Say that it is an area comparison, not a group rate.
- **Group rates from local data.** Use vital records, hospital discharge data or your own survey, broken out by group. Map the rates side by side with the same color scale.
- **Overlay.** Show where a group lives (ACS) on top of a health outcome map. Do not read the overlap as a group rate.

## Asset maps and primary data

The template's optional Appendix E is a local supplement for regional or multi-jurisdictional assessments. It asks you to "Identify local assets, relationships, resources, or strengths relevant to the findings" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)). Asset maps show clinics, food pantries, senior centers and partner sites next to need.

Maps can also gather primary data. The template says primary data are "newly collected specifically to inform the health assessment" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)).

They are collected "by, for, or under the direction of" a partnership member ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)). Resident mapping sessions, where people mark printed maps, can qualify if you record them as part of the assessment.

The template also warns that existing surveillance or program data "do not automatically qualify as primary population-health data" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)). Do not count an existing dashboard as your primary data.

## Doing the base maps faster

Desktop GIS such as QGIS or ArcGIS Pro is the better choice when you join local vital records or survey data to custom boundaries. For the federal base layers, a hosted tool can save time.

[GeoChat AI](https://app.geochat.ai) is an AI-powered healthcare access mapping platform for community health assessments and needs assessments. As of November 2026, it has [ACS 2020–2024, CDC PLACES 2025 release, HRSA shortage area and health center layers, and NPPES provider counts](/methods). They cover county, ZCTA and census tract levels. It has a free plan with no credit card required.

As of November 2026, its AI chat has no web search. It is instructed to report only figures from its own queries of the platform's datasets. That is an instruction to the model, not a guarantee. The platform is new, and sources are rebuilt by hand after each release. GeoChat AI is not affiliated with GeoChat, the MBZUAI remote-sensing model, or GeoChat, the InSTEDD messaging tool.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the template as the standard.** The template is a draft aid. Check required elements in PHAB's [Standards & Measures for Initial Accreditation 2026](https://phaboard.org/resource-library/standards-measures-for-initial-accreditation-2026/).
- **Maps with no finding.** The template warns against "demographic tables without explaining what they mean" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)). The same applies to maps: put a one-sentence finding above each.
- **Calling an area pattern a group disparity.** A PLACES map by tract is not a rate for a racial or ethnic group.
- **Counting NPPES registrations as clinicians.** Label provider maps as registered providers, not full-time staff.
- **Skipping limitations.** The template asks you to describe "missing data, small numbers, response bias, timeliness, geographic limitations" ([template](https://phaboard.org/wp-content/uploads/FINAL_Health-Assessment-Template-v2026.docx)). Note suppressed PLACES areas and large ACS margins of error on the map itself.
- **Mixing years without saying so.** PLACES and ACS cover different years. Put both in each map's source note.

## Next steps

1. Download PHAB's [templates](https://phaboard.org/resource-library/health-assessment-improvement-planning-templates/) and the Standards & Measures, and list the required elements a–f.
2. Choose one map geography and agree on it with partners, including hospitals planning CHNAs. See the [CHNA access gap mapping guide](/chna-access-gap-mapping-guide).
3. Build the population and health status maps from ACS and PLACES.
4. Build primary care and behavioral health access maps from HRSA and NPPES.
5. Pick two subpopulations early and find local data broken out by group.
6. Plan resident mapping sessions as part of your primary data collection.
