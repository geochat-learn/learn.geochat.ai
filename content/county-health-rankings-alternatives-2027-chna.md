Replace County Health Rankings in a 2027 CHNA with its federal sources: CDC PLACES, CDC WONDER, HRSA's Area Health Resources Files and Census ACS. No single site replaces it one for one. County Health Rankings itself [says](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/findings-and-insights/blog/faq-chrr-program-status-and-funding) it knows of no other site offering the same county-level data and resources.

## What changed at County Health Rankings

County Health Rankings & Roadmaps (CHR&R) is a program of the [University of Wisconsin Population Health Institute](https://countyhealthrankings.github.io/welcome/about.html) (UWPHI). Its status changed in four steps:

| Date | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | The 2025 Annual Release was the final annual release. | [CHR&R FAQ](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/findings-and-insights/blog/faq-chrr-program-status-and-funding) |
| January 26, 2026 | CHR&R said its foundation funding ends in December 2026. "2026 will be the last year that CHR&R exists in its current form." | [CHR&R FAQ](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/findings-and-insights/blog/faq-chrr-program-status-and-funding) |
| March 30, 2026 | CHR&R updated 34 measures but made no 2026 Annual Data Release, no National Findings Report and no 2026 Health Groups (formerly "Rankings"). | [CHR&R blog](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/findings-and-insights/blog/chrr-updates-data-for-34-measures-will-not-produce-a-2026-annual-data-release) |
| October 20, 2026 | UWPHI announced new multi-year funding. Core county-level data for every US county will remain available, but the funding "will not extend CHR&R as it exists today." | [UWPHI announcement](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/findings-and-insights/blog/uwphi-secures-new-multi-year-funding-to-evolve-chrr-beyond-2026) |

The October announcement adds a focus on Wisconsin and Minnesota. It names no funder and gives no date for a 2027 data release. Plan your 2027 CHNA as if no new national rankings will arrive in time.

The IRS requires a hospital CHNA [every three years](https://www.irs.gov/node/61871), with a written report that describes the data sources used. It names no required data source. Losing County Health Rankings does not break compliance; it changes where you get the numbers.

## Step 1: Save the County Health Rankings files now

CHR&R [says the website stays up through December 2026](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/findings-and-insights/blog/chrr-updates-data-for-34-measures-will-not-produce-a-2026-annual-data-release) and urges users to download data and documentation before then. The [Data & Documentation page](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/health-data/methodology-and-sources/data-documentation) offers:

- the 2025 national analytic data (CSV and SAS), data dictionary and technical document;
- the March 2026 supplemental release, with its own documentation;
- 2024 and 2025 trends files;
- 2025 state downloads for each state and DC.

Save the technical document too. You will need it to describe the method in your CHNA report.

UWPHI also runs an [Open Source Project](https://countyhealthrankings.github.io/welcome/about.html). Its stated aim is a community that uses CHR&R's "validated code, methods, and inputs to calculate and update measures." It also aims to keep long-term access to past releases.

## Step 2: Know the data years you already have

Each CHR&R measure page lists its data years, and many measures were updated after the 2025 release:

| Measure | 2025 release used | Updated to | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poor or fair health | 2022 BRFSS | 2023 data, released March 25, 2026 (Pennsylvania and Kentucky counties stay on 2022) | [CHR&R](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/health-data/population-health-and-well-being/quality-of-life/life-satisfaction/poor-or-fair-health) |
| Primary care physicians | 2021 | 2022 data, released September 24, 2025 | [CHR&R](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/health-data/community-conditions/health-infrastructure/clinical-care/primary-care-physicians) |
| Premature death | 2020–2022 | 2021–2023 data, released November 4, 2025 | [CHR&R](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/health-data/population-health-and-well-being/length-of-life/life-span/premature-death) |

In the report, cite these years, not "2026 County Health Rankings." There are no 2026 Health Groups to cite.

## Step 3: Rebuild the measures from the original sources

Many CHR&R measures come from federal files you can open directly. These four cover much of a typical CHNA data section.

### Health status and behaviors: CDC PLACES

[CDC PLACES](https://www.cdc.gov/places/about/index.html) gives model-based estimates for counties, places, census tracts and ZIP Code Tabulation Areas (ZCTAs). The [2025 release has 40 measures](https://www.cdc.gov/places/methodology/index.html), built from BRFSS, Census population counts and ACS 5-year data.

PLACES covers areas with [50 or more adult residents](https://www.cdc.gov/places/about/index.html). CHR&R's poor or fair health measure [already uses PLACES county estimates](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/health-data/population-health-and-well-being/quality-of-life/life-satisfaction/poor-or-fair-health). PLACES is the stronger choice when you need tract or ZCTA detail.

The estimates come from a [multilevel regression model](https://www.cdc.gov/places/methodology/index.html), not a local survey. Report the 95% confidence intervals PLACES publishes.

### Mortality: CDC WONDER

CHR&R's premature death measure uses [NCHS mortality and natality files](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/health-data/population-health-and-well-being/length-of-life/life-span/premature-death). [CDC WONDER](https://wonder.cdc.gov/wonder/help/ucd-expanded.html) lets you query county death counts and rates by cause.

WONDER [suppresses counts of 1 to 9 deaths](https://wonder.cdc.gov/wonder/help/ucd-expanded.html). It does not display rates whose 95% confidence interval is wider than 160% of the rate. In small counties, pool several years to get stable rates.

### Clinicians: HRSA Area Health Resources Files

CHR&R's primary care physician ratio comes from the [Area Health Resources Files (AHRF)](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/health-data/community-conditions/health-infrastructure/clinical-care/primary-care-physicians). HRSA's [AHRF](https://data.hrsa.gov/topics/health-workforce/ahrf) has more than 6,000 county, state and national variables, released annually.

To match the CHR&R method, count non-federal MDs and DOs under 75 in [general practice, family medicine, internal medicine or pediatrics](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/health-data/community-conditions/health-infrastructure/clinical-care/primary-care-physicians). Then divide the county population by that count. See [provider-to-population ratio](/glossary/provider-to-population-ratio) for how to read the result, and [HPSA](/glossary/hpsa) for HRSA's shortage designations.

### Insurance and social factors: Census ACS

For uninsured rates, use ACS 5-year table S2701. In the 2024 5-year data, variable [S2701_C05_001E](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/subject/groups/S2701.html) is the percent uninsured of the civilian noninstitutionalized population. ACS 5-year tables are published for counties, tracts and ZCTAs; see [ACS 5-year estimates](/glossary/acs-5-year-estimates) and [ZCTA vs. ZIP code](/glossary/zcta-vs-zip-code).

## Other dashboards CHR&R points to

CHR&R's own FAQ [lists](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/findings-and-insights/blog/faq-chrr-program-status-and-funding) America's Health Rankings, City Health Dashboard, CDC WONDER and PLACES as resources that may help. Each fills a different part of the gap:

| Tool | Geography | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| [America's Health Rankings](https://www.americashealthrankings.org/publications/reports/2025-annual-report/introduction) (United Health Foundation) | The nation, 50 states and DC; 99 measures from 31 sources in the 2025 report | State rankings and benchmarks |
| [City Health Dashboard](https://cityhealthdashboard.com/faq) (NYU Langone Health) | Cities, and tracts within them where the source data allow; over 40 measures | Urban hospitals and city health departments |
| [CDC PLACES](https://www.cdc.gov/places/about/index.html) | Counties, places, tracts, ZCTAs | Health status below the county level |
| [CDC WONDER](https://wonder.cdc.gov/wonder/help/ucd-expanded.html) | States and counties | Mortality by cause |

America's Health Rankings is the better choice for comparing your state with others. City Health Dashboard is the better choice for a city-focused CHNA, since it reports data within city limits.

## Where GeoChat AI fits

[GeoChat AI](https://atlas.geochat.ai) is a free mapping platform for community health needs assessments. As of November 2026, its GeoChat Atlas (version 2) includes [CDC PLACES (2025 release), ACS 2020–2024 5-year estimates, HRSA HPSAs and NPPES provider counts](/methods). It maps them at county, ZIP code (ZCTA) and census tract level.

As of November 2026, its plain-language AI chat can compare counties or drill into ZIP codes. The chat has no web search. The AI is instructed to report only figures from the platform's own datasets; that is an instruction to the model, not a guarantee. NPPES counts are registrations, not full-time clinicians. The platform is new; GeoChat Atlas is version 2. GeoChat AI does not reproduce CHR&R's Health Groups. GeoChat AI is not affiliated with GeoChat, the MBZUAI remote-sensing model, or GeoChat, the InSTEDD messaging tool.

## Common mistakes

1. **Citing "2026 rankings."** There were [none](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/findings-and-insights/blog/chrr-updates-data-for-34-measures-will-not-produce-a-2026-annual-data-release). Cite the release and each measure's data years.
2. **Waiting to download.** CHR&R [has said the current website stays up through December 2026](https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/findings-and-insights/blog/faq-chrr-program-status-and-funding). The October announcement gives no details on future downloads. Save the files and technical document now.
3. **Mixing methods silently.** If you rebuild a measure from AHRF or PLACES, say so. Your ratio may differ from CHR&R's because of data year or definitions.
4. **Treating model estimates as counts.** PLACES values are [modeled estimates](https://www.cdc.gov/places/methodology/index.html) with confidence intervals.
5. **Ignoring suppression.** Small-county death counts under 10 are [suppressed in WONDER](https://wonder.cdc.gov/wonder/help/ucd-expanded.html). Pool years rather than reporting zero.

For a full method, see the [CHNA access gap mapping guide](/chna-access-gap-mapping-guide) and the [free tools roundup](/free-tools-healthcare-access-mapping).
